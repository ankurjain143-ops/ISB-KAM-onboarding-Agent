# Day 8 – Costing & Commercial Basics

**Pillar:** Processes (Costing) | **Owner:** Finance + Commercial
**Learning goal:** The KAM understands how a price is built, what drives margin, how price changes and payment terms work – **knowledge only; no live pricing yet.**

> Structure and numbers are **[Illustrative – Team 8]** unless tagged otherwise.

---

## 1. Piece-price build-up

| Cost element | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Raw material | Metals, plastics, magnets, electronics – net weight plus scrap allowance |
| Bought-out parts | Components bought from sub-suppliers (e.g. PCBs, motors, springs) |
| Conversion cost | Machine hours, labour, power, consumables |
| Overheads | Plant overheads, quality, engineering support |
| Packaging and logistics | Customer-specific packaging, freight to delivery point |
| Rejection / warranty provision | Expected scrap and warranty risk |
| SG&A | Selling, general and administrative costs |
| Profit margin | Target margin as set by Finance for the product line |
| **= Piece price** | |

## 2. One-time costs
- **Tooling:** Moulds, dies, fixtures. Either paid by the customer upfront (customer-owned tooling) or **amortised** into the piece price over an agreed volume.
- **Development / engineering (NRE):** Design, prototypes, testing, validation – quoted separately or amortised.
- **Testing and certification** where required by the customer.

## 3. Margin logic
- Finance sets a **target margin** and a **floor price** for each quote.
- Every price below target needs approval as per the Day 6 approval matrix.
- Volume matters: a lower price may be acceptable on higher volume, but only Finance can model it.
- Beware of "one-way" concessions – if you give on price, ask for something back (volume, payment terms, tooling payment, longer contract).

## 4. Price changes during the contract
- **Raw-material indexation:** Many OEM contracts adjust price periodically (e.g. quarterly) based on published indices for steel, aluminium, copper or plastics.
- **Forex:** Imported components may carry an exchange-rate clause.
- **Annual price reduction (APR / productivity give-back):** OEMs often expect 1–3% price reduction per year; it should be negotiated in the annual price discussion, not conceded ad-hoc.
- **Engineering changes:** Cost impact of an ECN is quoted and approved like a new quote.

## 5. Payment terms and commercial terms
- **Payment terms** (e.g. 45, 60 or 90 days) directly affect working capital; any change needs CFO approval (Day 6).
- **Incoterms** (e.g. Ex-Works, FCA, DAP) decide who pays freight and who carries risk in transit.
- **Price validity:** Every quotation must state how long the price is valid.
- **Debit notes** from customers (for quality issues, late delivery) must be validated with Quality/SCM before acceptance.

## 6. Negotiation preparation (for Days 22–25, practised on Day 10)
1. Know your cost sheet, target margin and walk-away (floor) price – from Finance.
2. Know the customer's alternatives (competition, in-sourcing).
3. Prepare trade-offs in advance (what you can offer vs. what you need).
4. Agree the negotiation mandate with the KAM Head **before** the meeting.
5. Never agree a number in the meeting that exceeds your mandate – say you'll confirm.

## 7. Legal and ethical boundaries
[Source: PVNA HR Manual, p.19–20, 27–28]
- Never discuss prices, terms of sale, customers or suppliers with competitors – collusion is illegal.
- Unfair pricing practices, price discrimination and secret rebates are regulated by competition law; report questionable practices to HR.
- No kickbacks: giving back part of a deal's value to a buyer is prohibited.
- All discounts must be documented and reflected accurately in company records.
