# Day 3 – Plant Walk: Process Flow, Safety, Traceability & Logistics

**Pillar:** Processes | **Owner:** Plant + SCM
**Learning goal:** The KAM understands how a part moves from supplier to customer, what safety rules apply on the shop floor, and how traceability supports customer complaints and audits.

> Plant-specific details in this file are **[Illustrative – Team 8]** unless marked otherwise. PVNA has manufacturing facilities including new plants at Pune and Gurgaon [Source: padminivna.com].

---

## 1. Plant visit safety rules
[Illustrative – Team 8, aligned to the HR Manual's "safe place to work" principle, p.18]

- Carry your ID badge and sign in at security. Visitors and new joiners are escorted.
- Wear the PPE issued at the gate: safety shoes, safety glasses, and ear protection in marked zones. No loose clothing or jewellery near machines.
- Walk only on marked pedestrian aisles; forklifts have right of way.
- No photos or videos inside the plant without written approval – customer parts and processes are confidential.
- Never touch equipment, parts or controls. Report any unsafe condition to the supervisor or HR.
- Mobile phone use is restricted on the shop floor.
- Substance abuse and intoxication at work are strictly prohibited [Source: PVNA HR Manual, p.18].

## 2. Process flow – supplier to customer
[Illustrative – Team 8]

1. **Incoming (SCM + Quality):** Raw material and bought-out parts received, checked against the Goods Receipt Note and inspected (incoming quality).
2. **Stores:** Stored with identification labels, issued to the line on FIFO basis.
3. **Machining / moulding:** Components such as housings, shafts and valve bodies are machined, die-cast or moulded.
4. **Sub-assembly and final assembly:** Motors, actuators, sensors and valves assembled – typically on poka-yoke (error-proofed) stations.
5. **End-of-line (EOL) testing:** 100% functional testing, e.g. leak test, flow test, electrical test, calibration.
6. **Final inspection and packing:** Customer-specific packaging and labels (part number, lot number, barcode/QR).
7. **Dispatch (Logistics):** Shipped against customer schedules; ASN (advance shipping notice) where the customer requires it.

## 3. Critical operations a KAM should know
[Illustrative – Team 8]

| Operation | Why a KAM cares |
|---|---|
| EOL testing | Most customer quality issues are caught (or missed) here. |
| Leak / flow testing of valves | Critical to emission-control function. |
| Special characteristics (safety/regulatory) | Flagged on drawings; any change needs customer approval. |
| Calibration of sensors/actuators | Drives field performance and warranty. |

## 4. Traceability
[Illustrative – Team 8]

Every finished part or pack is traceable to its production date, shift, line, operator station, test results and raw-material lot. When a customer reports a problem, traceability lets PVNA:
- identify the suspect lot quickly,
- contain it (block stock in plant, warehouse and in transit),
- give the customer a clear "clean point" – the first good lot shipped after the fix.

A KAM should always ask for **part number, lot/batch number and date code** when a customer reports a complaint.

## 5. Logistics basics
[Illustrative – Team 8]

- Customers issue delivery schedules (monthly with weekly/daily call-offs).
- Delivery performance is tracked as **OTIF** (On Time In Full).
- Premium freight (air or urgent road transport) costs money – it needs approval (see Day 6 approval matrix) and should be avoided through early warning.
- Changes in customer schedules must be passed to Planning/SCM immediately.

## 6. Quality mindset
[Illustrative – Team 8]

Quality is everyone's job. If you see or hear about a potential defect, escalate the same day – never promise a customer that "everything is fine" without Quality's confirmation.
