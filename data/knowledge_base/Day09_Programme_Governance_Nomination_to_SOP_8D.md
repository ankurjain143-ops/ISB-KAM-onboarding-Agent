# Day 9 – Programme Governance: Nomination to SOP, and the 8D Method

**Pillar:** Governance (Programme & Quality) | **Owner:** Programme + Quality
**Learning goal:** The KAM understands what happens after PVNA wins a business (nomination) until regular production (SOP), and how customer problems are solved using 8D.

---

## 1. From nomination to SOP
[Industry standard + Illustrative – Team 8]

When a customer awards a part to PVNA, it issues a **nomination letter / LOI (letter of intent)**, followed by purchase orders. The programme then follows the APQP phases (Day 4).

| Milestone | What it means | KAM's role |
|---|---|---|
| Nomination / LOI | Business awarded; price and volumes agreed | Hand over to Programme Manager with all quote assumptions |
| Programme kick-off | Internal team, timeline, risks | Share customer milestones and contacts |
| Design freeze | Customer and PVNA agree final design | Ensure all customer changes are formally captured |
| Tooling kick-off (T0) | Tools ordered / made | Confirm tooling PO and payment terms with customer |
| Prototype / off-tool samples | Samples sent for customer testing | Track sample dispatch and customer feedback |
| PPAP submission and approval | Proof of production readiness | Coordinate dates; chase customer approval |
| Pilot / pre-series production | Low-volume builds at customer | Monitor early issues daily |
| SOP (start of production) | Regular serial supply begins | Transition to steady-state account management |
| Post-SOP monitoring (typically 3 months) | Early-production containment, ramp-up | Watch quality, delivery and schedules closely |

**Programme reviews** [Illustrative – Team 8]: fortnightly internal review led by the Programme Manager; monthly review with the customer's programme team. The KAM attends both.

## 2. Engineering change during a programme
Every change after design freeze follows change control (Day 4): impact on cost, timing and validation is assessed and formally agreed with the customer. The KAM must ensure changes are **priced** (Day 8) – unpriced changes erode margin.

## 3. The 8D problem-solving method
[Industry standard]

8D is the structured method most OEMs require for customer complaints.

| Discipline | Content |
|---|---|
| D0 | Prepare – symptoms, emergency response |
| D1 | Form the team (cross-functional) |
| D2 | Describe the problem (what, where, when, how many – "is / is not") |
| D3 | Interim containment actions (sort, block, replace) |
| D4 | Root cause – for both **occurrence** (why it was made) and **escape** (why it wasn't caught) – using 5-Why, fishbone |
| D5 | Choose and verify permanent corrective actions |
| D6 | Implement and validate corrective actions |
| D7 | Prevent recurrence – update FMEA, control plan, standards; apply to similar parts |
| D8 | Recognise the team and close |

**Typical timelines** [Illustrative – Team 8; follow the customer's own requirements]: D3 containment within 24 hours; D4 root cause within 7 days; full 8D closure within 30 days.

## 4. KAM's role in an 8D
- Make sure the customer receives D3 containment on time.
- Review the 8D before it goes out: is it clear, factual and complete?
- Keep the customer updated – silence damages trust more than bad news.
- Do not speculate on root cause before Quality confirms.
