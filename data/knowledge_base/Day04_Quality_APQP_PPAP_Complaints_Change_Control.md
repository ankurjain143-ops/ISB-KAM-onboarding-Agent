# Day 4 – Quality Governance: APQP, PPAP, Complaints & Change Control

**Pillar:** Governance (Quality) | **Owner:** Quality
**Learning goal:** The KAM understands the quality frameworks OEMs expect, how customer complaints are handled, and why no product or process change can be made without customer approval.

---

## 1. Why quality governance matters to a KAM
[Source: padminivna.com – About Us]

PVNA's reputation rests heavily on quality – it won GM's Supplier Quality Excellence Award five years running (2013–2017) and HMSI's Quality Management Award (FY20). Customers judge the KAM on how well quality issues are communicated and closed.

## 2. APQP – Advanced Product Quality Planning
[Industry standard – AIAG APQP]

APQP is the structured process for developing a new part so it is right first time. Its five phases:

| Phase | What happens | KAM's role |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Plan & define | Customer requirements, targets, feasibility | Capture complete RFQ requirements (Day 7) |
| 2. Product design & development | Design, DFMEA, prototypes | Relay customer design inputs and changes |
| 3. Process design & development | Process flow, PFMEA, control plan | Track timeline vs. customer milestones |
| 4. Product & process validation | Trial runs, capability studies, PPAP | Coordinate PPAP submission dates |
| 5. Launch, feedback & corrective action | SOP and ramp-up | Monitor early-production issues |

## 3. PPAP – Production Part Approval Process
[Industry standard – AIAG PPAP]

PPAP is the evidence package showing the production process can consistently make parts meeting all requirements. The customer must approve PPAP before regular supplies (SOP).

- **Key elements include:** design records, engineering change documents, customer engineering approval, DFMEA, process flow diagram, PFMEA, control plan, measurement system analysis (MSA), dimensional results, material/performance test results, initial process studies, qualified laboratory documentation, appearance approval (if applicable), sample parts, master sample, checking aids, customer-specific requirements, and the **Part Submission Warrant (PSW)**.
- **Submission levels 1–5** decide how much is sent to the customer; **Level 3** (full submission with samples) is the usual default.
- **Re-PPAP** is needed for any change to design, material, process, location or sub-supplier.

## 4. Customer-specific quality requirements
[Illustrative – Team 8]

Each OEM has its own requirements (reporting formats, PPM targets, audit schedules, escalation levels). The KAM must know them for their account – they are stored in the account's Customer 360 (Days 11–12).

Common metrics:
- **PPM** (defective parts per million supplied)
- Number of customer complaints per month
- Response time to a complaint (containment within 24 hours is typical)

## 5. Customer complaint handling
[Illustrative – Team 8, aligned to industry practice]

1. **Receive** – Customer reports an issue to the KAM or directly to Quality.
2. **Log within the same day** – The KAM logs it with Quality: part number, lot/date code, quantity, defect description, photos, where found (incoming, line, field).
3. **Contain within 24 hours** – Quality blocks suspect stock (plant, warehouse, in-transit), arranges sorting at the customer if needed.
4. **Investigate** – Root cause analysis using the 8D method (covered on Day 9).
5. **Respond** – Quality prepares the 8D; the KAM reviews it and shares it with the customer on the agreed timeline.
6. **Close** – Customer accepts the corrective actions; effectiveness is verified.

**KAM rule:** Never admit liability, accept a debit note or agree on compensation without Quality and the Reporting Boss (see Day 6 approval matrix).

## 6. Engineering change control
[Industry standard + Illustrative – Team 8]

- **Customer-initiated change:** The customer sends an ECN/ECR (engineering change notice/request). The KAM routes it to Engineering, gets feasibility, cost and timing impact, and returns a formal response through the quotation process.
- **Supplier-initiated change (PCN – process change notification):** Any change PVNA wants to make (material, sub-supplier, process, location, tooling) must be notified to and **approved by the customer before implementation**, usually with re-PPAP.
- Unapproved changes are one of the most serious quality violations in the industry and can lead to supplier blacklisting.
