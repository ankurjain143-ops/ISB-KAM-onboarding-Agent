## Scenario 1 – Mock RFQ

**Situation:** Aravalli Motors' purchase team emails an RFQ for an Electronic Throttle Valve for their new compact SUV (project "Falcon"). Annual volume 60,000; SOP in 18 months; quotation due in 12 working days. The drawing revision in the RFQ pack is Rev B, but the specification document refers to Rev C.

**What would you do?**

**Model answer:**
1. Acknowledge the RFQ the same day and log it in CRM (Day 7, step 1).
2. Raise the drawing-revision mismatch as a clarification immediately – never quote on an unclear revision.
3. Check completeness: lifetime volumes, test specifications, packaging, Incoterms, tooling terms.
4. Arrange a Go/No-Go with the KAM Head and Engineering within 2 days.
5. Hold the kick-off; set internal deadlines working backwards from day 12.
6. Get the quotation approved as per the Day 6 matrix before submitting.

**Scoring points for the mentor:** same-day acknowledgement; spots revision mismatch; follows the 12-step flow; does not give an indicative price.

## Scenario 2 – Pricing challenge

**Situation:** In a meeting, Aravalli's purchase manager says: "Your quote is 8% above your competitor. Match their price today and the business is yours."

**What would you do?**

**Model answer:**
- Stay calm and thank them for the feedback. Do **not** agree a number – an 8% reduction is beyond any KAM's authority and needs CEO-level approval if below floor (Day 6).
- Ask questions: Is the comparison like-for-like (tooling, packaging, Incoterms, validation scope, payment terms)? What volume commitment and timeline are they offering?
- Explain value: quality record, R&D support, emission-control expertise, supply reliability.
- Commit to a response date after reviewing internally with the KAM Head and Costing.
- Never ask for, or use, the competitor's confidential quote details (Code of Conduct – industrial espionage, p.28).

**Scoring points:** no on-the-spot concession; clarifies scope; gives a response date; stays within ethics.

## Scenario 3 – Customer quality escalation

**Situation:** At 7:30 pm, Aravalli's line quality engineer calls: 40 throttle valves from yesterday's delivery are failing at their assembly-line test. Their line may stop tomorrow morning.

**What would you do?**

**Model answer:**
1. Get the facts: part number, lot/date code, quantity affected, failure mode, photos.
2. Inform Customer Quality and the Plant immediately – this is an **L3 escalation** (line-stop risk) under Day 6; inform the KAM Head the same evening.
3. Ensure containment within 24 hours: block suspect stock in plant, warehouse and in transit; arrange sorting at the customer's end and replacement parts.
4. Tell the customer what is being done and when they'll hear next – without admitting liability or agreeing compensation.
5. Track the 8D (Day 9): D3 containment confirmed, D4 root cause within the agreed time.

**Scoring points:** gathers traceability data; escalates correctly and the same day; contains; no liability or compensation commitments.

## Scenario 4 – Gift request (ethics check)

**Situation:** A customer buyer hints that an expensive festival gift would "help" the upcoming nomination.

**Model answer:** Politely decline. Gifts must never be given to win or retain business, must be nominal in value and never cash [Source: PVNA HR Manual, p.19–20]. Inform your manager. Report the pressure via ethics@padminivna.com if it continues.
