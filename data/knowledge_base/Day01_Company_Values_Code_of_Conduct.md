## 1. About PVNA Group
[Source: PVNA HR Manual, p.5; padminivna.com – About Us]

- **Founded:** 1991, as Padmini Engineering, with the goal of doing both R&D and manufacturing in India. First facility in New Delhi; first product was automotive horns.
- **2005:** Padmini VNA Mechatronics came into existence after the horn business was divested to Hella.
- **Group companies:** Padmini VNA Mechatronics, PV Clean Mobility Technologies and Padmini E-Drive – offering mechatronics, electronics and clean-mobility technologies.
- **Scale & reach:** Over 30% of the workforce works in R&D; 100+ patents; about 8% of annual turnover invested in R&D; presence across Europe, Asia, North America and South America.
- **What we make:** High-efficiency components for passenger cars, commercial vehicles, two-wheelers, three-wheelers and tractors – including EGR valves, throttle bodies, vacuum modulation valves, pumps, solenoid valves, electronics, sensors, seating system and fuel system products.
- **Customers:** Leading OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers, in India and internationally.
- **Milestones:** GM Supplier Quality Excellence Award (2013–2017); HMSI Quality Management Award (FY20); JV with TT Electronics (2008–2013) for sensors (WSS, APS); range of xEV components developed (2015–2020); thermal management and electric powertrain products, new plants at Pune and Gurgaon, and certified India's Best Workplaces in Auto & Auto Components 2024 (2020–2024).

## 6. Code of Conduct – what every KAM must know
[Source: PVNA HR Manual, p.16–30]

The Code applies to employees, suppliers, customers, agents and consultants acting for the company.

**Anti-corruption.** Never offer, directly or indirectly, anything of value to a government official, customer or their representative to obtain or retain business, influence a decision or gain an unfair advantage. This includes bribes, kickbacks (a share of the deal returned to the buyer) and facilitation payments. PVNA is also responsible for third parties acting on its behalf.

**Gifts and entertainment to customers** are allowed only if the gift is: not meant to win business or an improper advantage; lawful and permitted under the customer's own policies; a genuine goodwill or promotional expense; not cash; and of nominal value, individually and in total.

**Conflict of interest.** Avoid outside employment with competitors, customers or suppliers; don't do company business with relatives or their firms; declare relationships at work; avoid financial interests in competitors, customers, suppliers or distributors.

**Confidential information.** All non-public company and customer information is confidential and covered by the NDA you sign on joining. Customer data (drawings, volumes, prices) must not be disclosed without the customer's consent, ideally in writing, and must never be used for personal advantage – even after you leave.

**Fair competition (critical for KAMs).** Never discuss prices, terms of sale, customers or suppliers with competitors – collusion is illegal. Don't make false statements about competitors. Report questionable practices such as secret rebates or unfair pricing to HR. Don't obtain or use competitors' confidential information.

**Media and social media.** Only the CEO/MD and Corporate Communications speak for the company. Don't post company product or business information online unless authorised. Refer press enquiries to Corporate Communications.

**Company assets and expenses.** Business expenses must be authorised by your manager before they are incurred; personal expenses are not reimbursed. Protect laptops and data; report loss within 24 hours.

**Personal conduct.** No intoxication while representing PVNA, including at a customer's or supplier's premises. Dress and behave professionally – to many customers, you are the only "PVNA" they will ever meet.

**Raising concerns.** Report violations to your manager, skip-level manager, HR or **ethics@padminivna.com**. PVNA does not tolerate retaliation against anyone reporting in good faith.

**Consequences.** Violations can lead to warnings, suspension, loss of variable pay, demotion or termination. Failing to report a known violation or failing to attend required training are also violations.
